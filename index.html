Why currents decide the operation

It is not just the surface. Currents run through the whole water column — and through your whole operation.

Wind and waves are felt at the surface. Currents act on everything below it — the lay catenary, the touchdown point, the ROV, the diver, the DP system, and the seabed itself. Get the current picture wrong and the work window, the touchdown, and the burial all get harder.

The same current field that slows a transit also bends the lay catenary, shifts the touchdown, loads the ROV and the DP thrusters, and — on the seabed — drives the scour that exposes cables. Standard current products miss most of this fine structure. Our model is built to resolve it.

The data gap

Wind and wave forecasting is mature. Ocean-current forecasting hasn't reached the same standard — until now.

Wind models run at near-operational accuracy; wave models follow from wind. But surface currents are a different problem: eddies are roughly ten times smaller than atmospheric storms (≈100 km vs ≈1,000 km), and there are far fewer ocean measurements to anchor the models. The result is positioning errors of tens of nautical miles — enough to flip a routing or work-window decision.

In Amphitrite's own survey of ship captains, just 7% rated ocean-current forecasts reliable, against 70% for wind. The two leading operational models (EU Copernicus and US NOAA) routinely disagree on the location and strength of the same current vein on the same day. Misplacing a vein by 30–50 nm changes the decision entirely.

Where it fits

Where it fits into offshore & subsea operations.

Route-to-site planning & mobilisation
Plan transit and arrival against wind, waves, and 3.0 km currents — so the vessel arrives on time and in workable condition, not just departs on time.
Workability windows (DSV, crane & construction vessels)
Go/no-go decisions depend on wave height, period, and current speed together. Get accurate windows days in advance — not wind-only estimates.
DP-sensitive operations
Current is a primary environmental load on station-keeping. Accurate site-current data reduces thruster demand, sharpens DP capability planning, and extends operable windows in tidal and eddy-rich areas.
Cable lay, burial & subsea installation
Currents bend the catenary, shift the touchdown, and load the product during lay — and drive seabed scour that later exposes cable. Forecast currents along the whole route at 3.0 km, not just at waypoints.
ROV & diving operations
ROV flying, tether management, and diver safety all fight the current. Better current forecasts mean better go/no-go and fewer aborted dives.
Survey & geophysics
Tow-fish, AUV and sensor performance and survey-line planning depend on current. Undetected currents cause re-runs and lost survey days.
Heavy lift, tow & rig-move planning
Loaded semi-sub and jacket tows are highly sensitive to current forces across the hull and towline. Forecast along the entire tow corridor, not just endpoints.
Offshore wind installation
Jack-up and DP installation spreads live and die by workability. Tighter combined wind/wave/current windows reduce standby and weather downtime.

Custom vessel model

A model of your specific vessel — for the most accurate route-to-site and tow planning.

Generic vessel-type assumptions introduce systematic routing error. A custom model learns how your vessel responds to every combination of wind angle, wave direction, and current — trained on your own AIS and onboard-sensor history (position, STW, SOG, heading, COG; roll/pitch, hull dimensions, power curves). The output is a performance polar: speed gain or loss at every angle and sea state — and on custom-trained models we reach under 5% MAPE.

Head seas and adverse currents cost speed (red); following seas and favourable currents add it (green). For offshore work that means realistic transit ETAs, honest tow-speed planning, and current-aware fuel and emissions estimates — trained from your vessel's own data.
